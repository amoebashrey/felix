Our approach

Every decision lands in one of three buckets.

Support reps always know what to expect. Engineers always know what Felix already tried.

01
Resolve

Felix answers it himself.

A confident knowledge-base match. Felix posts the fix inline, cites the section, and asks for a 👍 or 👎.

Trigger
Similarity above 0.65 against a KB section.
Outcome
Support rep reacts. Decision logged to Supabase.
02
Ask

Felix needs one more thing.

A likely match with one piece of context missing. Felix asks exactly one question, scoped to what would unlock the answer.

Trigger
A match between 0.45 and 0.65, or two sections tied.
Outcome
Support rep replies. Felix re-evaluates, then resolves or escalates.
03
Escalate

Felix hands it off cleanly.

No match, or incident words like 5xx, "down," "broken for everyone." Felix routes to the right team with a one-line summary, then steps back.

Trigger
No KB match, or an incident signal in the message.
Outcome
Frontend takes over with full context. Felix exits.

The problem

Engineers were drowning. Support reps were waiting.

Every tech blocker went to whoever was around. The same questions came back weekly, and the ones that needed engineering landed on the wrong team, stripped of context.

3→1 hops to the right team
01 · REPEATS

Repeat issues drain engineering time.

The top 20 questions in #tech-problems are the same every week, answered by hand, one thread at a time.

02 · ROUTING

Wrong-team routing wastes hours.

An SMS bug pings the CRM team, a dashboard issue goes to infra. Hours pass before the right person even sees it.

03 · CONTEXT

Context evaporates on escalation.

Repro steps, lead IDs, error messages: all buried three scrolls up by the time an engineer picks up the thread.

FAQ

Frequently asked.

01Is Felix replacing engineers?

No. Felix handles the repetitive stuff, the same twenty questions support reps ask every week. Engineers get more time for the work that needs engineering. The escalation path is faster, not absent.

02What happens if Felix is wrong?

Support reps react with 👎. That's a signal: the KB entry needs editing, or the pattern is new and needs an entry. Wrong answers get logged, reviewed, and either fixed or used to bootstrap a new KB section.

03Why not use a generic AI bot?

A generic bot hallucinates domain-specific answers and routes everything to whoever looks like an "engineer." Felix only resolves on a real KB match. When it doesn't know, it asks one question or escalates.

04How is the KB maintained?

Today: hand-authored from real resolution threads, reviewed before publishing. Next quarter: 👎 reactions auto-generate draft entries, and a lightweight admin UI means KB edits don't need a PR.

05What data does Felix log?

Every triage decision: the original message, the path chosen, the KB section cited, the reaction, and any follow-up. Stored in Supabase. Used to compute resolution rate per domain, false-positive rate, and which KB sections pull their weight.
